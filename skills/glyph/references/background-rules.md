# Backgrounds and surfaces

Load only when relevant. Requirement = correctness/accessibility obligation; Pattern = adaptable starting point; Taste hypothesis = design judgment to validate in context.

### Choose from the content
* **Confidence Level**: Taste hypothesis
* **Purpose**: Support readability and relevant personality.
* **Reasoning**: Category-based background recipes can become another skin.
* **Constraint**: Select white, black, tinted, photographic, illustrated or textured surfaces according to brand, content and task. Do not force warm paper, charcoal or a fixed palette on every product. A requested pure-white or pure-black base is valid.
* **Example**:
  * **Bad**: Every developer product must be dark and monospaced.
  * **Good**: Follow the established identity and inspect text, focus and semantics on actual surfaces.
* **Exceptions**: Patterns may change when the product, user constraints or observed evidence justify it. Requirements remain.

### Layer without obscuring
* **Confidence Level**: Pattern
* **Purpose**: Make surfaces distinguishable where needed.
* **Reasoning**: Tint, borders, spacing and elevation can all establish hierarchy.
* **Constraint**: Choose the minimum surface differentiation that makes roles clear. Gradients, patterns, noise and glass are optional. Check text over all image/scroll states. Avoid global blur or fixed opacity recipes as substitutes for measured contrast.
* **Example**:
  * **Bad**: A translucent header disappears over a changing photo.
  * **Good**: Use a sufficiently opaque header surface and verify the scrolled state.
* **Exceptions**: Patterns may change when the product, user constraints or observed evidence justify it. Requirements remain.

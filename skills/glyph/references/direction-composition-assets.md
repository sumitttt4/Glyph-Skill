# Direction, composition and assets

Load only when relevant. Requirement = correctness/accessibility obligation; Pattern = adaptable starting point; Taste hypothesis = design judgment to validate in context.

### Compare meaningful directions
* **Confidence Level**: Taste hypothesis
* **Purpose**: Find a fitting composition.
* **Reasoning**: Palette swaps are not independent design directions.
* **Constraint**: For open-ended work compare distinct hierarchies, densities and asset strategies. Choose with reasons, then compose the important screen with actual content. Preserve established direction unless redesign is requested.
* **Example**:
  * **Bad**: The same centered hero in three colors.
  * **Good**: Compare a workbench walkthrough, editorial field guide and example-led gallery.
* **Exceptions**: Patterns may change when the product, user constraints or observed evidence justify it. Requirements remain.

### Choose a personality carrier
* **Confidence Level**: Taste hypothesis
* **Purpose**: Make the design specific without adding everything.
* **Reasoning**: Photography, typography, illustration and interaction have different strengths.
* **Constraint**: Decide which carrier fits the content and what supporting roles are needed. Check original content before creating filler assets. Keep UI readable and purposeful.
* **Example**:
  * **Bad**: Apply portfolio inline logos and rotating greetings to a banking dashboard.
  * **Good**: Use the dashboard's real information hierarchy and relevant data visualization.
* **Exceptions**: Patterns may change when the product, user constraints or observed evidence justify it. Requirements remain.

### Use references responsibly
* **Confidence Level**: Requirement
* **Purpose**: Keep work original and attributable.
* **Reasoning**: Public visibility does not grant redistribution rights.
* **Constraint**: Check creator, source, license and permitted use. Learn composition principles without tracing artwork or copying a reusable skin. Do not treat displayed logos or metrics as verified facts. Label concepts and fixtures.
* **Example**:
  * **Bad**: Reuse a reference's customer logos and trace its illustration.
  * **Good**: Create original explanatory graphics and accurately attributed real examples.
* **Exceptions**: Patterns may change when the product, user constraints or observed evidence justify it. Requirements remain.

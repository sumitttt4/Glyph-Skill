# Landing-page reference research

Load only when relevant. Requirement = correctness/accessibility obligation; Pattern = adaptable starting point; Taste hypothesis = design judgment to validate in context.

### Research the question, not the trend
* **Confidence Level**: Pattern
* **Purpose**: Find a composition that explains this product.
* **Reasoning**: Beautiful screenshots do not establish conversion or product fit.
* **Constraint**: Write audience, promise, primary action, available evidence and visual problem before browsing. Compare a bounded set of meaningfully different approaches, then stop when the composition decision is supported. Skip fresh research for routine fixes.
* **Example**:
  * **Bad**: Search best SaaS and copy the first hero.
  * **Good**: Compare a process demonstration, editorial explanation and real output gallery for an invisible design tool.
* **Exceptions**: Patterns may change when the product, user constraints or observed evidence justify it. Requirements remain.

### Translate observation into a decision
* **Confidence Level**: Pattern
* **Purpose**: Transfer reasoning rather than borrowed components.
* **Reasoning**: A source's imagery and layout solve its own content problem.
* **Constraint**: Inspect actual renders, not only snippets. Record URL/date, what was visible, focal hierarchy, section order, roles of type/color/assets/motion, what to transfer, what to reject and access limits. Archive screenshots do not demonstrate mobile behavior or live interaction.
* **Example**:
  * **Bad**: Use Raycast style and assume it converts better.
  * **Good**: Transfer one focal hierarchy principle into an original explanation; reject the source palette, artwork and claims.
* **Exceptions**: Patterns may change when the product, user constraints or observed evidence justify it. Requirements remain.

### Compose around evidence
* **Confidence Level**: Pattern
* **Purpose**: Make the product understandable before decoration.
* **Reasoning**: A compulsory section sequence can hide the primary task.
* **Constraint**: Use actual product screens or a labeled illustrative walkthrough. Place installation or the primary action near its explanation. Choose remaining section order from visitor questions, not a mandatory hero/logos/bento/testimonials template. Do not invent proof.
* **Example**:
  * **Bad**: Hide install beneath scenery and fake reviews.
  * **Good**: Show a concise promise, copyable command and interactive explanation.
* **Exceptions**: Patterns may change when the product, user constraints or observed evidence justify it. Requirements remain.

### Keep rights separate from inspiration
* **Confidence Level**: Requirement
* **Purpose**: Preserve originality and truthful reporting.
* **Reasoning**: A public library is not a redistribution license.
* **Constraint**: Do not bundle proprietary screenshots or copy artwork/code without permission. Check adaptation licenses and retain notices. Design preferences are hypotheses, not measured findings. Do not bypass access controls, buy a subscription or install a scraper as an implicit dependency.
* **Example**:
  * **Bad**: Download a paid gallery wholesale.
  * **Good**: Keep source links and original notes; build original assets and report access limits.
* **Exceptions**: Patterns may change when the product, user constraints or observed evidence justify it. Requirements remain.
